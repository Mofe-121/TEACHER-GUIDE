# TEACHER-GUIDE
Guide for designing an AI-IBED-aligned instruction design

This guide shows you how to plan a science lesson that is built around inquiry, tied to standards, and supported by AI in a responsible way. It follows Understanding by Design (backward design): you start with the big idea and the assessment evidence, and only then plan the 5E learning sequence (Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate).

The guide walks through a finished Grade 6 lesson on air pressure. The lesson is aligned to Virginia SOLs 6.1 and 6.7, NGSS crosscutting concepts, and INTASC standards. Each row of the template comes with:

What it means: the purpose of each row in the lesson template
Look out for: common mistakes
Weak and strong examples: from the sample lesson
Standards check: so, the lesson meets the level of the standard it claims
Transferable method: so, you can apply the approach to any state's standards

AI use: prompts for tools like Claude, ChatGPT, and NotebookLM, with notes on where AI helps and where it gets things wrong.
The guide ends with an activity sheet, a rubric, and three self-audits covering standards alignment, inquiry integrity, and AI integration.

Who should use this guide?

Pre-service teachers: learn backward design and 5E planning, and prepare lesson plans that hold up in methods courses, field placements, and licensure reviews.
In-service teachers: tighten existing lessons, check that they really align to standards, and add AI to your planning without losing your own judgment as a professional.
Teacher educators: use it as a model and a teaching tool in methods courses, as a shared language for feedback, and as a starting point for talking about using AI responsibly in lesson design.
